Lesson 2: Understanding Unit Rates

Grade 6 Math | Ratios and Proportional Relationships

📚 Standard: 6.RP.2
⏱️ Duration: 60 minutes
🔗 Prerequisite: Lesson 1

✏️ STATUS: DRAFT – Ready for SME review

Learning Objectives

BASIC

  • Define a unit rate as "quantity per 1 unit"
  • Calculate a unit rate by dividing one quantity by another

INTERMEDIATE

  • Identify unit rates in real-world contexts (price per item, miles per hour)
  • Use unit rate language correctly and compare unit rates to make decisions

ADVANCED

  • Analyze and compare complex unit rates
  • Explain why equivalent ratios produce the same unit rate
  • Use unit rates to solve multi-step problems

Lesson Timeline

Hook/Warm-Up (8 minutes)

Activity: "Which is the Better Deal?"

Store A: 4 notebooks for $12
Store B: 3 notebooks for $9

Question: Which store has the better deal? Why?

Expected thinking: Students will divide to find cost per notebook

  • Store A: $12 ÷ 4 = $3 per notebook
  • Store B: $9 ÷ 3 = $3 per notebook

Transition: "Great thinking! You just found a UNIT RATE. A unit rate tells us the price for one notebook. Today we're learning how to find unit rates and why they help us make better decisions."

Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

Part A: Define Unit Rate

UNIT RATE = A ratio where the second quantity is 1

Examples:
- $3 per notebook (3 dollars for 1 notebook)
- 55 miles per hour (55 miles for 1 hour)
- 2 grams per serving (2 grams for 1 serving)

Part B: How to Calculate Unit Rate

Step 1: Identify the ratio

Problem: 12 miles in 2 hours
Ratio: 12 miles : 2 hours

Step 2: Set it up as a division

We want: ___ miles : 1 hour
So we divide:
12 miles ÷ 2 = 6 miles
2 hours ÷ 2 = 1 hour

Step 3: Write with "per"

Unit rate: 6 miles per hour (or 6 mph)

Key teaching point: "We divide BOTH parts of the ratio by the same number. We're trying to make the second quantity equal to 1."

Guided Practice (22 minutes)

Activity: Real-World Rate Comparison

Scenario: Gas Prices

Pump A: 12 gallons for $48
Pump B: 10 gallons for $40
Pump C: 15 gallons for $45

Together, calculate each unit rate:

Pump Calculation Unit Rate
Pump A $48 ÷ 12 $4 per gallon
Pump B $40 ÷ 10 $4 per gallon
Pump C $45 ÷ 15 $3 per gallon ✓ BEST

Conclusion: "Unit rates help us make smart decisions. Pump C is the cheapest because $3 per gallon is less than $4 per gallon."

Independent Practice (12 minutes)

1. A pack of 6 pens costs $3.
Unit rate: ___ per pen

2. A car travels 240 miles in 4 hours.
Unit rate: ___ miles per hour

3. COMPARISON: Two juice brands
Brand A: 6 bottles for $9
Brand B: 8 bottles for $10
Which is better? Why?

Exit Ticket (3 minutes)

1. What is a unit rate?
________________________________

2. If 5 apples cost $2, what is the unit rate?
$____ per apple

Common Misconceptions

Misconception What students think How to address
Wrong division "If 4 books cost $12, which do I divide?" "Divide the money by the books: $12 ÷ 4. How much per ONE book?"
Forgetting "per" "The unit rate is just 3" "Always say PER: $3 PER book. The 'per' reminds us it's for ONE."
Answer doesn't make sense "A kid runs 100 mph" "Does that make sense? No! Let me recalculate..."
