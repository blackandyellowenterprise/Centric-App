Lesson 3: Solving Ratio Problems

Grade 6 Math | Ratios and Proportional Relationships

📚 Standard: 6.RP.3
⏱️ Duration: 60 minutes
🔗 Prerequisites: Lessons 1-2

✏️ STATUS: DRAFT – Ready for SME review

Learning Objectives

BASIC

  • Solve simple one-step ratio problems
  • Use one strategy (table, diagram, or equation) to find missing quantities

INTERMEDIATE

  • Solve multi-step ratio problems with real-world context
  • Select and justify an appropriate strategy for given problem
  • Use tables, tape diagrams, or number lines to solve problems

ADVANCED

  • Solve complex ratio problems using multiple representations
  • Explain why different strategies yield the same answer
  • Create word problems for given ratio solutions

Lesson Timeline

Phase Time Activity
Hook 8 min Recipe scaling scenario
Direct Instruction 18 min Three solution strategies
Guided Practice 22 min Practice with support
Independent Practice 9 min Student choice of strategy
Exit Ticket 3 min Reflect on strategies

Hook/Warm-Up (8 minutes)

Scenario: Party Punch Recipe

A recipe makes punch for 4 people and uses:
- 2 cups orange juice
- 3 cups ginger ale
- 1 cup lime juice

But we have 12 people coming!
How much of each ingredient do we need?

Ask students:

  • "How many times bigger is 12 than 4?" → 3 times
  • "So how much orange juice do we need?" → Let them think
  • "How would you figure this out?"

Transition: "Today you're going to learn multiple ways to solve ratio problems. Different strategies work for different problems, and you'll pick which one makes the most sense."

Direct Instruction (18 minutes)

Teach three main strategies for solving ratio problems:

Strategy 1: Using a Table of Equivalent Ratios

People Orange Juice Ginger Ale Lime Juice
4 2 cups 3 cups 1 cup
8 4 cups 6 cups 2 cups
12 6 cups 9 cups 3 cups

Explain: "I made a table to show equivalent ratios. When I multiply people by 3 (4×3=12), I multiply ingredients by 3 (2×3=6)."

Strategy 2: Using a Tape Diagram

FOR 12 PEOPLE (3 units, since 12÷4=3):

Orange Juice: [2][2][2] = 2+2+2 = 6 cups
Ginger Ale: [3][3][3] = 3+3+3 = 9 cups

Key idea: "The tape diagram shows the ratio as a bar. We're repeating it 3 times."

Strategy 3: Using Multiplication

4 people → 12 people (multiply by 3)

OJ: 2 × 3 = 6 cups
Ginger Ale: 3 × 3 = 9 cups
Lime Juice: 1 × 3 = 3 cups

Key idea: "Multiply both parts of the ratio by the same number."

Important: "All three strategies give the SAME answer. You pick the one that makes sense to YOU for that problem."

Guided Practice (22 minutes)

Problem 1: Map Scale

On a map, 2 inches = 10 miles. If two cities are 8 inches apart on the map, how many miles apart are they in real life?

Map inches Real miles
2 10
4 20
8 40

Answer: 40 miles

Problem 2: Running Speed

Maya ran 15 miles in 3 hours. At this rate, how far will she run in 9 hours?

Notice: 9 ÷ 3 = 3
So multiply distance by 3: 15 × 3 = 45 miles

Problem 3: Recipe Scaling

A smoothie recipe uses 6 strawberries for every 2 cups yogurt. If you use 5 cups yogurt, how many strawberries do you need?

Method 1: Unit Rate
6 strawberries ÷ 2 cups = 3 strawberries per cup
5 cups × 3 = 15 strawberries

Independent Practice (9 minutes)

PROBLEM 1: Mixing Paint
A painter mixes 3 cans red with 5 cans blue paint. If she uses 12 cans red, how many cans blue?

PROBLEM 2: Running Club
Four members ran 7 miles in 1 hour. How far in 4 hours?

PROBLEM 3: Photo Printing
A shop charges $6 for 8 photos. How much for 32 photos?

CHALLENGE: Which recipe uses more eggs per person?
Recipe A: Serves 6 people, uses 2 eggs
Recipe B: Serves 8 people, uses 3 eggs

For each problem, write: What strategy did you use? Why?

Exit Ticket & Closure (3 minutes)

Which strategy would you use for each problem?
(Table / Tape Diagram / Multiplication)

1. A car travels 240 miles in 4 hours. How far in 6 hours?
Strategy: ________________
Why: ________________________

2. A recipe is for 2 people. You need to feed 10 people.
Strategy: ________________
Why: ________________________

Closing: "Great work! You learned three different ways to solve ratio problems. Tomorrow, we'll use what you learned to solve bigger problems and show your thinking."
