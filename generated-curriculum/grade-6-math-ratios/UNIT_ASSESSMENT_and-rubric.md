## PART A: Multiple Choice (20 points)
**Time:** 15 minutes | **6 questions × ~3-4 points each**

**Directions:** Choose the best answer. Circle the letter.

---

### Question 1 (6.RP.1 - BASIC)
**In a classroom, there are 15 boys and 10 girls.**
**What is the ratio of boys to girls?**

A) 10:15
B) 15:10
C) 10 to 25
D) 15:25

**Correct Answer:** B (15:10)

**Distractors explain:**
- A: Reversed order
- C: Mixed form, still wrong ratio
- D: Boys to total students (not boys to girls)

---

### Question 2 (6.RP.1 - INTERMEDIATE)
**Which three expressions all represent the same ratio?**

A) 3:1, 3 to 1, 3/1
B) 3:1, 1:3, 3/1
C) 3:1, 3 to 3, 3/3
D) 3:1, 1 to 3, 1/3

**Correct Answer:** A (all represent 3:1)

**Key concept:** Three different ways to write the same ratio

---

### Question 3 (6.RP.1 - ADVANCED)
**A recipe uses 2 cups flour to 1 cup sugar. If you increase the recipe proportionally, what would be true?**

A) The ratio would become 2:2
B) The ratio would stay 2:1
C) The ratio would change to 1:2
D) You would only need 1 ingredient

**Correct Answer:** B (ratio stays the same when scaled proportionally)

**Key concept:** Equivalent ratios maintain the same relationship

---

### Question 4 (6.RP.2 - INTERMEDIATE)
**A runner ran 20 miles in 4 hours. What is the unit rate (miles per hour)?**

A) 4 miles per hour
B) 5 miles per hour
C) 20 miles per hour
D) 24 miles per hour

**Correct Answer:** B (20 ÷ 4 = 5)

**Calculation:** 20 miles ÷ 4 hours = 5 miles per hour

---

### Question 5 (6.RP.2 - ADVANCED)
**Two stores have different unit prices:**
**Store A: 8 notebooks for $16**
**Store B: 6 notebooks for $12**

**Which store has the better price per notebook?**

A) Store A ($2 per notebook)
B) Store B ($2 per notebook)
C) Both stores have the same price
D) Cannot determine without more information

**Correct Answer:** C (Both $2 per notebook)

**Calculation:**
- Store A: $16 ÷ 8 = $2 per notebook
- Store B: $12 ÷ 6 = $2 per notebook

---

### Question 6 (6.RP.3 - INTERMEDIATE)
**A recipe for 4 people uses 3 cups of rice. How much rice is needed for 12 people?**

A) 6 cups
B) 7 cups
C) 9 cups
D) 12 cups

**Correct Answer:** C (9 cups)

**Reasoning:** 12 people is 3 times 4 people, so 3 cups × 3 = 9 cups

## PART B: Short Answer (30 points)
**Time:** 20 minutes | **Three questions requiring work and explanation**

---

### Question 1 (6.RP.2 - Worth 10 points)

**Three coffee shops have different drink sizes:**
- Shop A: 12 oz for $3
- Shop B: 16 oz for $4
- Shop C: 20 oz for $5

**For each shop, find the unit rate (price per ounce).**
**Then determine which shop offers the best value.**

**Expected Response:**
```
Shop A: $3 ÷ 12 oz = $0.25 per oz
Shop B: $4 ÷ 16 oz = $0.25 per oz
Shop C: $5 ÷ 20 oz = $0.25 per oz

All three shops have the same unit price.
```

**Scoring Rubric:**
- Correct calculation for all 3 (6 pts)
- Correct identification that all are equal (2 pts)
- Shows work clearly (2 pts)

### Question 2 (6.RP.3 - Worth 10 points)

**A paint mixture uses 2 parts red paint to 3 parts blue paint.**

**If you have 10 gallons of red paint, how much blue paint do you need?**

**Show your work using a table, diagram, or equation.**

**Expected Response (Multiple ways shown):**

**Table Method:**
| Red | Blue |
|-----|------|
| 2   | 3    |
| 4   | 6    |
| 6   | 9    |
| 8   | 12   |
| 10  | 15   |

**Equation Method:**
Ratio is 2:3, so blue = red × (3/2)
Blue = 10 × (3/2) = 15 gallons

**Tape Diagram Method:**
Red: [5 groups of 2] = 10 gallons
Blue: [5 groups of 3] = 15 gallons

**Scoring Rubric:**
- Correct answer (15 gallons) (5 pts)
- Clear strategy/method (3 pts)
- Shows complete work (2 pts)

## PART C: Performance Task (50 points)
**Time:** 40-50 minutes | **Integrated application task**

### Performance Task: Plan a Class Party

**Scenario:**

You're planning a math class party for 24 students. You need to make decisions about quantities and costs.

**Task A (15 points): Calculate Quantities Using Ratios**

A recipe for lemonade serves 6 people and uses:
- 4 lemons
- 2 cups sugar
- 6 cups water

Your class has 24 people. Calculate how much of each ingredient you'll need for the party.

**Show your work using at least TWO different strategies (table, tape diagram, number line, or equation).**

**Expected Response:**
Since 24 ÷ 6 = 4, multiply each ingredient by 4:
- Lemons: 4 × 4 = 16 lemons
- Sugar: 2 × 4 = 8 cups
- Water: 6 × 4 = 24 cups

**With two strategies demonstrated**

**Scoring:**
- Correct calculations (8 pts)
- Two clear strategies shown (5 pts)
- Work is organized and easy to follow (2 pts)

---

**Task B (15 points): Compare Unit Rates and Make a Decision**

Your teacher asks about buying cookies for the party.

Three bakeries have different prices:
- Bakery A: 18 cookies for $9
- Bakery B: 24 cookies for $12
- Bakery C: 15 cookies for $6

**1) Calculate the unit rate (price per cookie) for each bakery.**

**2) Which bakery offers the best value? Justify your answer.**

**3) If you need 72 cookies for the party, how much will it cost at the cheapest bakery?**

**Expected Response:**
```
Bakery A: $9 ÷ 18 = $0.50 per cookie
Bakery B: $12 ÷ 24 = $0.50 per cookie
Bakery C: $6 ÷ 15 = $0.40 per cookie

Bakery C is cheapest at $0.40 per cookie.

For 72 cookies: 72 × $0.40 = $28.80
```

**Scoring:**
- Correct unit rates (6 pts)
- Correct identification of best value (4 pts)
- Correct final calculation (3 pts)
- Clear reasoning (2 pts)

---

**Task C (20 points): Create Your Own Ratio Scenario**

Design your own ratio problem for the class party. Your problem should:
- Include a real-world context (decorations, supplies, costs, quantities)
- Require at least one ratio calculation
- Use at least one unit rate
- Include a decision or comparison

**Write:**
1) Your problem statement (what are we trying to figure out?)
2) Necessary information (quantities, prices, relationships)
3) Solution showing your work
4) Answer with explanation

**Example Response:**
```
Problem: We need balloons for decorations. Balloons come
in packages of 20 for $5. How much will 100 balloons cost?

Information:
- 20 balloons = $5
- Need: 100 balloons

Solution:
Unit rate: $5 ÷ 20 = $0.25 per balloon
100 × $0.25 = $25

Answer: 100 balloons will cost $25.
```

**Scoring Rubric:**
- Problem is clear and realistic (6 pts)
- Information is sufficient (3 pts)
- Solution is mathematically correct (6 pts)
- Explanation is clear (5 pts)

## PROFICIENCY RUBRIC

Use this rubric to assess overall performance and assign proficiency levels.

### BASIC (Below Grade Level - 60-70%)
**Student can:**
- Identify and write ratios in one form
- Calculate simple unit rates with support
- Solve one-step ratio problems using one strategy

**Performance indicators:**
- May confuse ratio notation (3:1 vs. 1:3)
- Relies on teacher scaffolds
- Work is sometimes disorganized
- Calculations are often correct, but explanations are weak

---

### INTERMEDIATE (At Grade Level - 71-85%)
**Student can:**
- Write ratios in multiple forms
- Calculate unit rates correctly
- Solve multi-step ratio problems using a clear strategy
- Make decisions based on unit rates

**Performance indicators:**
- Notation is correct and consistent
- Chooses appropriate strategy independently
- Work is organized and can be followed
- Explanations are clear (though not elaborate)

---

### ADVANCED (Above Grade Level - 86-100%)
**Student can:**
- Analyze ratios in complex contexts
- Calculate and compare multiple unit rates
- Solve multi-step problems using multiple strategies
- Justify why different strategies yield the same answer
- Create accurate problems for peers

**Performance indicators:**
- Writes ratios correctly in all forms
- Selects most efficient strategy for given problem
- Work is organized, clear, and uses appropriate vocabulary
- Explanations show deep understanding
- Can articulate reasoning and generalize patterns
