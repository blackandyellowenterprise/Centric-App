# Lesson 2: Understanding Unit Rates
**Unit:** Ratios and Proportional Relationships | **Grade:** 6 | **Duration:** 60 minutes

**Standard(s):** 6.RP.2 – Understand the concept of a unit rate and use rate language

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

### BASIC
- Define a unit rate as "quantity per 1 unit"
- Calculate a unit rate by dividing one quantity by another

### INTERMEDIATE
- Identify unit rates in real-world contexts (price per item, miles per hour)
- Use unit rate language correctly and compare unit rates to make simple decisions

### ADVANCED
- Analyze and compare complex unit rates
- Explain why equivalent ratios produce the same unit rate
- Use unit rates to solve multi-step problems

## Detailed Lesson Plan

### PHASE 1: Hook/Warm-Up (8 minutes)

**Objective:** Connect ratios from Lesson 1 to real-world rates

**Activity: "Which is the Better Deal?"**

**Present two store scenarios:**

```
Store A: 4 notebooks for $12
Store B: 3 notebooks for $9
```

**Ask students (think-pair-share):**
- "Which store has the better deal? Why?"
- "How would you figure this out?"
- "What math are you using?"

**Expected thinking:**
- Some may divide: $12÷4 = $3 per notebook
- Some may divide: $9÷3 = $3 per notebook
- Some may recognize both are the same

**Transition:**
"Great thinking! You just found a **UNIT RATE**. A unit rate tells us the price for **one** notebook. Today we're learning how to find unit rates and why they help us make better decisions."

---

### PHASE 2: Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

**Objective:** Explicitly teach unit rate concept and calculation

#### Part A: Define Unit Rate (5 minutes)

**Write on board/chart:**
```
UNIT RATE = A ratio where the second quantity is 1

Examples:
- $3 per notebook (3 dollars for 1 notebook)
- 55 miles per hour (55 miles for 1 hour)
- 2 grams per serving (2 grams for 1 serving)
```

**Say:**
"A unit rate tells us how much of something we have **for each one** of another thing. The word 'per' means 'for each' or 'for one.'"

**Give relatable examples:**
- "If I run 12 miles in 2 hours, my unit rate is 6 miles **per** hour. That means I run 6 miles in **one** hour."
- "If 4 notebooks cost $12, the unit rate is $3 **per** notebook. That means **one** notebook costs $3."

---

#### Part B: How to Calculate Unit Rate (7 minutes)

**Show the process step-by-step:**

**Step 1: Identify the ratio**
```
Problem: 12 miles in 2 hours
Ratio: 12 miles : 2 hours
```

**Step 2: Set it up as a division**
```
We want: ___ miles : 1 hour
So we divide: 12 miles ÷ 2 = 6 miles
              2 hours ÷ 2 = 1 hour
```

**Step 3: Write the unit rate with "per"**
```
Unit rate: 6 miles per hour (or 6 mph)
```

**Do another example:**
```
Problem: 4 notebooks cost $12
Ratio: 4 notebooks : $12

Step 1: We want: 1 notebook : ___ dollars
Step 2: Divide both by 4:
        4 notebooks ÷ 4 = 1 notebook
        $12 ÷ 4 = $3

Step 3: Unit rate: $3 per notebook
```

**Key teaching points:**
- "We divide BOTH parts of the ratio by the same number"
- "We're trying to make the second quantity equal to 1"
- "The answer should make sense in the real world"

---

#### Part C: Using Unit Rates to Compare (3 minutes)

**Now solve the warm-up problem together:**

```
Store A: 4 notebooks for $12
Unit rate: $12 ÷ 4 = $3 per notebook

Store B: 3 notebooks for $9
Unit rate: $9 ÷ 3 = $3 per notebook

They're the SAME! Either store is equally good.
```

**Ask:**
"What if Store C had 6 notebooks for $15? Would that be a better deal?"
- Calculate: $15 ÷ 6 = $2.50 per notebook
- "Yes! $2.50 is less than $3, so Store C is the best deal."

### PHASE 3: Guided Practice (22 minutes)

**Objective:** Practice finding and comparing unit rates with support

#### Activity 1: Calculating Unit Rates (12 minutes)

**Set up a table on board/chart:**

| Scenario | Calculation | Unit Rate |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| 6 apples cost $3 | $3 ÷ 6 = | $0.50 per apple |
| Runner travels 20 miles in 4 hours | 20 ÷ 4 = | 5 miles per hour |
| 8 cookies for 2 servings | 8 ÷ 2 = | 4 cookies per serving |

**Do first example together:**
- "We have 6 apples for $3"
- "To find the unit rate, we divide: $3 ÷ 6 = $0.50"
- "So each apple costs $0.50, or 50 cents"

**Do second example with student help:**
- "What do we divide? 20 by 4"
- "Why divide by 4? Because we want 1 hour instead of 4 hours"
- "What's 20 ÷ 4? 5"
- "So the unit rate is 5 miles per hour"

**Do third example with students doing most of the work:**
- Teacher guides: "What's our ratio? 8 cookies to 2 servings"
- Students calculate: "8 ÷ 2 = 4"
- Students say: "The unit rate is 4 cookies per serving"

**Add 2-3 more examples:**
- 12 pencils for $4 → $4 ÷ 12 = $0.33 per pencil
- 15 push-ups in 3 minutes → 15 ÷ 3 = 5 push-ups per minute
- 200 calories in 4 servings → 200 ÷ 4 = 50 calories per serving

---

#### Activity 2: Real-World Rate Comparison (10 minutes)

**Scenario: Gas Prices**

Display prices at different gas stations:
```
Pump A: 12 gallons for $48
Pump B: 10 gallons for $40
Pump C: 15 gallons for $45
```

**Guide students through:**

1. **Calculate Pump A's unit rate:**
   - What's our ratio? 12 gallons : $48
   - What do we divide by? 12
   - $48 ÷ 12 = $4 per gallon

2. **Calculate Pump B's unit rate:**
   - Students do: 10 gallons : $40
   - Students calculate: $40 ÷ 10 = $4 per gallon

3. **Calculate Pump C's unit rate:**
   - Students do: 15 gallons : $45
   - Students calculate: $45 ÷ 15 = $3 per gallon

4. **Compare and decide:**
   - "Which pump is the cheapest? Pump C!"
   - "How do we know? Because $3 per gallon is less than $4 per gallon"

**Why this matters:**
"Unit rates help us make smart decisions. If you're buying gas for your family's car, you'd want to choose Pump C because it saves you money!"

### PHASE 4: Independent Practice (12 minutes)

**Objective:** Apply unit rate skills without teacher support

**Activity: Unit Rate Practice Worksheet**

```
UNIT RATE PRACTICE
Name: ___________________  Date: __________

Find the unit rate for each situation.

1. A pack of 6 pens costs $3.
   Unit rate: ___ per pen

2. A car travels 240 miles in 4 hours.
   Unit rate: ___ miles per hour

3. A recipe calls for 3 cups of flour for 2 batches of cookies.
   Unit rate: ___ cups per batch

4. A store sells 12 avocados for $8.40.
   Unit rate: $____ per avocado

5. COMPARISON: Two juice brands are on sale.
   Brand A: 6 bottles for $9
   Brand B: 8 bottles for $10

   Brand A unit rate: $____ per bottle
   Brand B unit rate: $____ per bottle

   Which is the better deal? ________
   How much cheaper per bottle? $________

6. CHALLENGE: A restaurant serves 5 hamburgers with 2 pounds of meat.
   If they need to serve 15 hamburgers, how many pounds of meat
   will they need? Show your thinking.
```

**Differentiation:**

**For struggling learners:**
- Provide problems 1-3 only
- Write the division problem they need to solve: "Divide ___ by ___"
- Allow use of calculator
- Partner with more confident student for problem 5

**For on-level learners:**
- Complete all 6 problems
- Use calculator if needed

**For advanced learners:**
- Problem 6 first (extension)
- Add: "If meat costs $8 per pound, how much will 15 hamburgers cost in meat?"
- Create their own unit rate problem

## Common Misconceptions & How to Address

| Misconception | What students think | What to say/show |
|---|---|---|
| **Wrong division** | "If 4 books cost $12, then 12÷4 tells us... wait, which way?" | "We divide the money by the books: $12 ÷ 4 books = $3 per book. Think: How much per ONE book?" |
| **Forgetting the 'per'** | "The unit rate is just 3" | "Unit rate always has 'per' in it: $3 PER book. The 'per' reminds us it's for ONE of something." |
| **Not checking if it makes sense** | "My answer is 100 miles per hour for a kid running" | "Does that make sense? No! A kid can't run 100 mph. Let me recalculate..." |
| **Confusing which quantity is 'per one'** | "If a recipe serves 2 people with 3 cups flour, it's 2÷3 = 0.67 cups per person" | "Good setup! 3 cups ÷ 2 people = 1.5 cups per person. Each person gets 1.5 cups." |

---

## Tips for Success

✅ **Use the word "per" constantly** – Makes unit rate language familiar

✅ **Emphasize the "1"** – "Unit rate means FOR EACH ONE of something"

✅ **Relate to their lives** – Price per item, miles per hour, points per game

✅ **Model thinking aloud** – Show hesitation, self-correction: "Wait, let me check if that makes sense..."

✅ **Use division consistently** – Show both formats: 12÷4 and in fraction form 12/4

✅ **Compare two options** – Students love deciding "which is better" – use this motivation
